# Methods

Companion to [../README.md](../README.md). Everything here is implemented in
`conductance/` and reproducible with `make all`.

---

## 1. Estimating the impact operator

The theory writes price change as `ΔP = G · ε`, with `ε` the shock and `G` the
conductance. `G` is not directly observable, but in a Kyle (1985) framework
`r = λ · flow`, so `λ` — price impact per unit of trade — *is* the object.

With daily free data the estimator is

```
log G_t = log |r_t| − α · log v_t
v_t     = V_t / median(V, trailing 252 days)
```

Three choices carry weight.

**Volume normalisation.** Dividing by a trailing median removes secular volume growth
and contract-roll drift without touching sub-annual variation. The window is trailing
only, so it introduces no lookahead.

**`α` is estimated, not assumed.** Regressing `log|r|` on `log v` over the full sample
gives α̂ = 1.032 for gold (R² = 0.176) — close to Amihud's (2002) linear assumption,
not the square-root law's 0.5. Estimates range 0.41 (euro) to 1.36 (S&P 500) across
the universe, so assuming a common exponent would have been wrong.

**Orthogonality.** At the estimated `α`, `log G` is orthogonal to `log v` by
construction, so persistence in `log G` cannot be an artefact of volume persistence.
`test_conductance_is_orthogonal_to_volume_at_estimated_alpha` verifies |corr| < 0.02
on synthetic data.

Results are reported for α̂, 0.5 (square-root law) and 1.0 (Amihud). All three agree.

## 2. The null, and why this estimator was chosen

Under the joint hypothesis that

- `G` is a constant, and
- volume and volatility co-move only through information arrival
  (the mixture-of-distributions hypothesis),

`log G_t` as defined above is **iid**. That is the reason for this particular
estimator: the null is a sharp, testable statement rather than a vague one.

The null distribution is generated by **iid bootstrap of the empirical `log G`** —
2,000 resamples, preserving the exact fat-tailed marginal while destroying any time
structure. The identical statistic pipeline runs on real and simulated series, so
anything the pipeline itself induces appears in the null too.

Statistics: AR(1), AR(5), AR(22), Ljung-Box(22), variance ratios at 5 / 22 / 66 days,
and out-of-sample R² of a HAR forecast (daily / weekly / monthly lags, expanding
window, refit monthly, benchmarked against the expanding mean).

**Calibration is tested, not assumed.** `tests/test_calibration.py` builds synthetic
markets with known ground truth — persistent volume in both arms, `G` constant in one
and an AR(1) state variable in the other — and checks the test does not over-reject
under the null and does detect the alternative.

## 3. Constructing signed Λ

```
Λ_down = MM_long  · w(underwater_long)  / absorption
Λ_up   = MM_short · w(underwater_short) / absorption
absorption = OI · breadth · (1 − top-4 concentration)
```

**Trigger distance** uses a cost-basis tracker: a weighted-average entry price that
moves toward the current price only when a position is *added to*, leaving it
unchanged when trimmed. Distance from basis is then scaled to a monthly volatility
horizon (`σ_daily · √22`, ≈ 4.7%, the order of CME's 5% gold margin rate) and passed
through a logistic.

> **Correction applied during the first run.** The initial version scaled by *daily*
> volatility (≈1%) while positions sit ≈10% from basis. This saturated the logistic
> to exactly 0 or 1, making Λ a binary switch with `Λ_asym` ranging ±20. It was fixed
> before the reported numbers were produced. Both versions fail their kill conditions;
> the fixed version fails less badly.

**Point-in-time discipline.** COT is a Tuesday snapshot released Friday 15:30 ET.
Every row stores reference date *and* release date; the daily join is
`merge_asof(..., left_on=date, right_on=release_date, direction="backward")`, so a
figure is never available before it was published.
`test_cot_merge_never_uses_unreleased_data` enforces this.

## 4. Regression specifications

**Kill condition 1.** Target is mean `log G` over `t+1 … t+h` for h ∈ {1, 5, 22}.
Two benchmarks: the one the theory names (realised + implied vol), and a stricter one
adding conductance's own HAR history. Newey-West standard errors with `max(2h, 10)`
lags. Out-of-sample R² uses an expanding window, refit monthly, against the expanding
mean.

**Kill condition 2.** Forward returns over `h` regressed on `log(Λ_down / Λ_up)`.

**Kill condition 3.** Probability of a 2σ move in the following five days, by quintile
of `Λ`.

**Per-variable COT diagnostic.** Each raw COT variable added individually to the full
control set (realised vol, implied vol, HAR terms). Construction-free: it does not
depend on how Λ was assembled.

## 5. The scheduled-event test

Kill condition 4 establishes that conductance is persistent. It cannot establish *why*.
Two stories fit equally well:

- **(A) conductance** — `G` is a transmission operator varying with market structure;
- **(B) clustered news** — `|ε|` is itself autocorrelated, so the persistence is news
  arrival, not transmission.

Scheduled FOMC decisions separate them. The date is fixed years ahead, so arrival
carries no information, and the surprise magnitude is close to unpredictable by
construction of an event study. Under **(B)** pre-event `G` should add nothing beyond
what implied volatility already prices for that day. Under **(A)** it forecasts the
transmission of whatever lands, with a coefficient near 1.

```
log|r_event| ~ β · log G_pre + γ₁ · log RV_pre + γ₂ · log IV_pre
```

`G_pre` and `RV_pre` are means over a strictly prior window `[t−25, t−6]`; `IV_pre` is
the last GVZ close before the event. The benchmark is deliberately hostile: GVZ is
forward-looking and already knows the meeting is coming.

Because ~148 events is too thin for an expanding window, out-of-sample uses
**leave-one-year-out** cross-validation. Robustness covers pre-event windows ending
at `t−2`, `t−6` and `t−11`; the result is stable across all of them.

A **placebo** runs the identical specification on all non-FOMC days.

## 6. Generalisation

The theory's own bar: *"If it only works on gold, it is a gold story dressed as a law
and should be discarded."* The KC4 measurement runs unchanged across nine further
instruments spanning equity, rates, credit, FX, energy, metals and emerging markets,
each with `α` re-estimated and its own iid null.
